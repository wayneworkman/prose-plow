Prose Plow runs small language models entirely on the phone. The models are packaged inside the app and run on the phone's processor through llama.cpp. Nothing is downloaded while the app runs and nothing leaves the phone: the app has no network permission. Every chat is stored encrypted behind a lock that cannot be switched off.

<b>Chats</b>
* Several chats, listed in a drawer.
* A model for each chat, which can be switched in the middle of the chat. Every answer is labelled with the model that wrote it.
* A reply can be stopped, asked for again, or continued after it was stopped or reached its length limit.
* An earlier message of yours can be edited; the model then answers the new text.
* In Settings, a page for each model: temperature, the longest reply, how far its context may grow, and a system prompt where the model was trained with one.
* Answers are shown as Markdown, with a copy button on every message. Images are never loaded, and a link is opened only after you confirm it.
* Messages are deleted after 7 days by default; the time can be changed, or set to never.

<b>Lock and encryption</b>
* The permissions are for fingerprint unlock; for a foreground service that finishes an answer out of sight and keeps the lock on time, with its notifications, which never show a word of a chat; for keeping the processor awake; and for keeping the background job that deletes expired messages scheduled after a restart.
* Chats, drafts and settings are only ever stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM).
* The lock is mandatory. The first start asks for a passphrase of at least 10 characters that is not only digits, so a PIN is refused.
* Unlocking is by that passphrase or, if you turn it on, by fingerprint, which cannot fall back to the phone's PIN, pattern or password.
* The app locks at every start and after the time in the background you choose (1 minute by default). An answer under way is finished behind the lock, for at most 10 minutes, unless you turn that off.
* What the model has read of the last conversation is saved, encrypted, so that after unlocking it need not read it all again. This can be turned off.
* A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered; "Reset app data" deletes everything.
* Screenshots and screen recordings are blocked. On Android 13 and later, "Allow screenshots for 10 minutes" lifts that for a while and is never stored.
* Every text field asks the keyboard not to learn from what is typed, which a keyboard may ignore.
* The app's data is in no backup. Uninstalling, resetting or losing the phone loses the chats.

<b>Models</b>
* Qwen3.5 0.8B (Apache-2.0): a general assistant in many languages.
* Peacebell v1 291M (Apache-2.0): answers questions about the Second World War from memory; not a general assistant.
* Boris-2-Instruct (Apache-2.0): a very small chat model, fluent and often wrong on facts.
* Shard-2 (MIT, as declared in its model card): not a chat model. It continues the text you write and can take back words it has just written.

Every model ships as 16-bit floats, never quantised to fewer bits, which costs size and speed. The APK is about 2.5 GB and needs a 64-bit ARM phone (arm64-v8a) with Android 9 or later. Only the processor is used, not the GPU. The app is text only.

<b>Limits</b>
Small models are often wrong. The chat screen says so at all times: "This app uses AI, and AI can make mistakes." Check anything that matters.

Speed depends on the phone. On the one phone it was tested on, a Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro, Qwen3.5 0.8B wrote about 9 tokens per second and Boris-2-Instruct about 52. Beyond what the model and the phone's memory can hold, the oldest messages are left out, and the app says so.

The lock does not protect against someone who knows the passphrase, against whoever holds the phone while the app is unlocked, or against a compromised phone. The code has had no independent security review.
