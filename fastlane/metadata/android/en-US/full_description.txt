Prose Plow runs small language models entirely on the phone. The models are packaged inside the app and run on the phone's processor through llama.cpp. Nothing is downloaded while the app runs and nothing is sent over the network: the app has no network permission. Every chat is stored encrypted behind a lock that cannot be switched off.

<b>Chats</b>
* Several chats, listed in a drawer. A chat can be deleted.
* A model for each chat, which can be switched in the middle of the chat. Every answer is labelled with the model that wrote it.
* A reply can be stopped while it is being written, asked for again, or continued after it was stopped or reached its length limit.
* One of your own earlier messages can be edited, which removes everything after it and has the model answer the new text.
* In Settings, a page for each model: a temperature from 0 to 2, the longest reply, how far its context may grow, and a system prompt for each model that was trained with one.
* Answers are shown as Markdown, with a copy button on every message. Images are never loaded, and a link is handed to another app only after you have confirmed it.

<b>Privacy</b>
* The permissions in the APK are for fingerprint unlock and for finishing an answer out of sight: a foreground service, its two notifications, which never show a word of a chat, and keeping the processor awake.
* Chats, drafts, the list of chats and the settings are only ever written to the phone encrypted (AES-256-GCM).
* The lock is mandatory. The first start asks for a passphrase of at least 10 characters that is not only digits, so a PIN is refused, and no setting turns the lock off.
* Unlocking is by that passphrase or, if you turn it on, by fingerprint. Fingerprint unlock cannot fall back to the phone's PIN, pattern or password.
* The app is locked at every start and after the time in the background that you choose (1 minute by default). An answer under way is finished behind the lock, for at most 10 minutes, unless you turn that off.
* There is no recovery. A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered; "Reset app data" deletes everything.
* Screenshots and screen recordings are blocked. On Android 13 and later, "Allow screenshots for 10 minutes" in Settings lifts that for a while and is never stored.
* Incognito keyboard: every text field asks the keyboard not to learn from what is typed, which a keyboard may ignore.
* The app's data is in no backup. Uninstalling the app, resetting it or losing the phone loses the chats.

<b>Models</b>
The models in this version, each with its licence:
* Qwen3.5 0.8B (Apache-2.0): a general assistant in many languages, and the default for a new chat.
* Peacebell v1 291M (Apache-2.0): answers questions about the Second World War from memory; not a general assistant.
* Boris-2-Instruct (Apache-2.0): a very small chat model, fluent and often wrong on facts.
* Shard-2 (MIT, as declared in its model card): not a chat model. It continues the text you write and can take back words it has just written.

Every model ships as 16-bit floats; nothing is quantised to fewer bits. That costs size and speed. With the models listed above the APK is about 2.5 GB, and it needs a 64-bit ARM phone (arm64-v8a) with Android 9 or later. Only the processor is used, not the GPU. The app is text only.

<b>Limits</b>
Small models are often wrong. The chat screen says so at all times, under its title bar: "This app uses AI, and AI can make mistakes." Check anything that matters.

Speed depends on the phone. On the one phone it was tested on, a Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro with Android 17, Qwen3.5 0.8B wrote about 9 tokens per second and Boris-2-Instruct about 52; other phones will differ. A model's context grows with the conversation as far as the model and the phone's memory allow; beyond that, the oldest messages are left out of the latest answer and the app says how many.

The lock protects what is stored on the phone. It does not protect against someone who knows the passphrase, against whoever holds the phone while the app is unlocked, or against a compromised phone. The code has had no independent security review.
